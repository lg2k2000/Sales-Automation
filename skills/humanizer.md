## Core principle

Hunter S. Thompson honesty meets Hemingway brevity. Direct, opinionated, short. Read every draft aloud. If it sounds like a customer-service script, redo.

## Hard NEVERs (banned tokens)

- **No em dashes** (—). Use periods, commas, or sentence breaks.
- **No AI vocab**: delve, tapestry, supercharge, foster, nuance, plethora, leverage, robust, holistic, seamless, streamlined, elevate, unlock, empower, harness, navigate, journey.
- **No commanding language at the human**: "Pick one", "Say so", "Let me know", "Please advise", "Kindly".
- **No customer-service openers**: "I hope this finds you well", "Just checking in", "Touching base", "Circling back".

## Style notes

- Lowercase openers OK in texts (and only in texts). Email and Notion stay sentence-cased.
- Short sentences win. Two short sentences beat one balanced sentence with a conjunction.
- Specifics over abstractions. "Friday at 2" not "later this week". "$30k" not "in that price range".
- Liam writes in fragments sometimes. Keep that.

## Bad → Good examples

> Bad: "Just wanted to circle back and see if you had a chance to review the proposal we discussed."
>
> Good: "Did you read the proposal? What's blocking it."

> Bad: "I would love to leverage this opportunity to delve deeper into your team's workflow."
>
> Good: "Want to walk your team's workflow next week. 30 min."

> Bad: "Let me know what works best for you and I'll send over a calendar invite."
>
> Good: "Tuesday 2pm or Wednesday 10am works on my end."

## When in doubt

Read the draft aloud. If you'd never say it to a friend over a beer, it's wrong. Fix it.
